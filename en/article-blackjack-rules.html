How to play blackjack: rules and card values

Blackjack looks dead simple: get close to 21 and do not bust. Then soft 17, splits, doubles, and insurance land on the table, and the wheels come off. I will walk through the game in plain English, so you know not only which button exists but what it actually does.

In short: your job is to beat the dealer without going over 21. You do not need exactly 21. A natural blackjack is an ace plus a ten-value card in the first two cards; it is different from a three-card 21.

The goal is to beat the dealer, not chase 21

A hand closer to 21 than the dealer wins, provided it does not go over. An 18 beats 17; even 12 wins when the dealer busts. I think dropping the “I must make 21” idea is the fastest beginner upgrade.

Over 21 is a bust and loses immediately. Equal final totals are a push, so the original bet is normally returned. No win, no damage.

Table talk: 20 is already a monster hand. Hitting it because “an ace might be next” is not brave—it is basically speedrunning a bust.

An ace acts like a shock absorber. A+6 is soft 17. Add a ten and the ace drops from 11 to 1, leaving 17 instead of a bust. This clicks much faster in a live example than in a wall of theory.

Hard handNo ace counted as 11. Examples: 10+6, or A+5+10, are hard 16.
Soft handAn ace still counts as 11. A+7 is soft 18, so one extra card cannot bust it.

How a round works

  1. The player places a practice bet and receives two cards.
  2. The dealer receives two cards, usually one face up and one face down.
  3. The player acts using their cards and the dealer's upcard.
  4. The dealer reveals the hole card and follows the table's fixed drawing rule.
  5. Remaining hands are compared.

The player acts first and can bust before the dealer moves. That order is a core source of the house edge. The dealer does not improvise or “feel lucky”; the rule printed on the table runs the show.

Hit, Stand, Double, Split, and Surrender

HitTake one more card. Repeat until you stand or bust.
StandKeep the current total and end your turn.
DoubleDouble the bet, receive exactly one card, then stand automatically.
SplitTurn a matching pair into two separately funded hands.

Surrender gives up the hand and usually returns half the bet, when offered. Insurance is a separate side bet available against a dealer ace. I would skip it as a beginner: the name sounds comforting, but the maths does not make it a safety net.

Natural blackjack versus a regular 21

A blackjack is specifically an ace plus a ten, jack, queen, or king as the first two cards. A+K is blackjack; 7+7+7 is simply 21. After splitting aces, A+10 is commonly treated as a regular 21. Always check the table rules.

Blackjack typically beats a multi-card 21 and receives a special payout. If both player and dealer have blackjack, the result is normally a push. “Even money” against a dealer ace is insurance wearing a different hat—not free value.

Dealer rules: S17 and H17

The dealer must hit 16 or less and stand on hard 17 or more. The fork is soft 17, such as A+6. S17 means stand; H17 means hit. H17 is slightly worse for the player.

S17 = dealer stands on soft 17 · H17 = dealer hits soft 17

I always check the felt before practising a chart. Deck count, double-after-split, surrender, and soft-17 rules can change the best decision. A basic-strategy chart without its rules is a map with no scale.

A sample hand, step by step

You hold 10+6 against a dealer 10: hard 16. Standing feels rough, but hitting can bust. Under common multi-deck rules, basic strategy says Hit because standing performs even worse on average.

A 4 arrives, making 20, so I stand. The dealer reveals 6, must hit 16, draws 8, and busts. If I had drawn a king and busted instead, the decision would not become wrong. A move is judged by its expected result before the card lands, not by one heater or one brick.

Five beginner leaks

  • Playing only against 21. You are playing against the dealer's final hand.
  • Copying the dealer. You have more choices and the disadvantage of acting first.
  • Buying insurance by reflex. It is another wager, not protection for the first one.
  • Ignoring 3:2 versus 6:5. The payout changes the game's maths.
  • Results-based thinking. One win does not prove a bad move was good.
My take: learn the mechanics, then drill basic strategy under one clear ruleset. Betting systems do not repair bad decisions or erase the house edge. Keep it calm; no chasing, no “this one is due” nonsense.

FAQ

What is the goal of blackjack?

Beat the dealer with a stronger hand without exceeding 21. Exactly 21 is not required.

How much is an ace worth?

An ace counts as 1 or 11, whichever produces the strongest valid hand.

Is blackjack the same as any 21?

No. A natural blackjack is an ace plus a ten-value card in the first two cards.

Does the dealer hit 17?

The dealer stands on hard 17. On soft 17, S17 means stand and H17 means hit.

Is 3:2 or 6:5 better?

3:2 is better: a 100 bet earns 150 instead of 120 for a natural blackjack.

Does basic strategy guarantee a win?

No. It improves decisions and can reduce the house edge, but it cannot guarantee a profitable session.
